# Book Foundations and Depth Plan

Status: proposed, not started. Prepared 2026-10-10 after the editorial round
`b722d59`, from a read-only re-reading of all 15 chapters with one question:
*where does the text assume a concept instead of teaching it, and where is an
explanation too short to carry a new reader?* Line numbers refer to `b722d59`.

This plan is complementary to [BOOK_EDITORIAL_PLAN.md](BOOK_EDITORIAL_PLAN.md),
which removed operational prose. That plan made the chapters cleaner; this one
makes them more complete. It adds explanatory material only. It does not add
chapters or days, change measured results, launch experiments, advance the
learner beyond Day 9, or authorize publication.

## 1. Why the text reads as thin

The chapters are precise, but they are written in the voice of a specification:
each paragraph states a rule, then a boundary. They rarely show a picture,
walk through a small number, or say why a design was chosen over its
alternative. The result is text that an expert can verify but a new reader
cannot learn from without outside help.

Four recurring patterns explain the impression of thinness:

1. **Concepts are named, not taught.** Teacher forcing, KL divergence,
   advantage, on-policy, chat template, weight decay, dropout, SDPA, BF16,
   bootstrap, Goodhart, soft targets: each is used before, or instead of, a
   definition for a reader who does not already know it.
2. **Derivations arrive whole.** The result is correct, but the step that would
   make it reproducible by hand is omitted (softplus identity, Lagrange step
   in DPO, Adam bias correction, the output-head gradient, the row gradient
   of attention).
3. **No small numbers.** Almost no formula is followed by a two- or
   three-element computation the reader can check with a pencil.
4. **Design choices are facts.** Divide by $\sqrt{d_h}$, pre-norm, RMSNorm,
   tie embeddings, AdamW, clip at 0.2, $\beta=0.1$, normalize by group std,
   $k_3$: stated without the failure of the alternative.

The preface (l.17–18) promises a reader who knows "Python, arrays, basic
derivatives and the broad idea of neural networks" and "need not have trained
a language model". The chapters currently assume more than that.

## 2. The explanatory unit

Every addition below follows one shape, inserted at the point of first use:

> **Plain statement** (one or two sentences of what the thing is and what it
> is for) → **picture or analogy** (one figure or one concrete image) → **tiny
> numeric example** (2–4 elements, every number shown) → **why this and not
> the alternative** (what breaks without it) → **where it returns** (forward
> pointer to the chapter that depends on it).

Units are 10–60 lines. They do not replace existing derivations; they precede
them. Figures are generated by small, committed Python scripts under
`notebooks/figures/` or `visuals/`, carrying their source and render command.
Every number in a numeric example is reproduced by a cell in the day's
notebook or by a test under `tests/`, so the prose never carries an unchecked
value.

Caveats remain, but one per section, usually at its end. Where a paragraph
currently ends with three boundary statements, keep the one a new reader
needs and move the rest to the evidence section or the solutions.

## 7. Working rules for the additions

- Insert at first use; never append a glossary section to the end of a
  chapter as a substitute for teaching the concept where it appears.
- Every numeric example is reproduced by a test or a notebook cell; name it.
- Every figure has a committed generator and render command.
- Keep existing derivations, exercises and evidence intact; add before them.
- One caveat per section unless the section is the evidence block.
- Link back to the single home of a concept rather than redefining it.
- Do not introduce new measured claims; use only numbers already in reports
  or computable from fixtures.

New chapters, days or notebooks beyond figure-generation cells; Spark

hosted CI; animation rendering; publication; learner position or mastery.
